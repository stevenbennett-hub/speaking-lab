Speak Up · The Speaking Lab

Find your voice. Use it for something.

A free, open public speaking course for high school and college-level learners: 16 weeks, five speeches, hands-on practice labs, and speeches that changed the world. In English and Spanish.

  • Learn how great speakers inform, persuade, and inspire.
  • Practice each skill with instant feedback.

Units

Readings, the week-by-week plan, the speech assignment for your level, slide decks, and example videos.

Practice Labs

Hands-on practice with feedback that explains why. Retry as often as you like; practicing never lowers your progress.

What am I learning?Research and source evaluation, outlining, and ethical persuasion: the skills behind every strong speech.
Why does it matter?Speakers earn trust with credible evidence and clear organization. These skills also help you judge what you read and hear every day.
How do I show mastery?Work through each activity until it is mastered, reflect on what you learned, and build an outline that passes the checklist before you submit it.

Voices of Change

Sixteen speeches that changed the world, about one a week, each matched to what we are learning. Watch it, read it, then analyze it with the same tools you use for your own speeches.

Rubrics

Every speech has a high school and a college-level rubric. Read it before you start; it is your checklist.

Pacing guide

Day-by-day plan for all 16 weeks, five class days a week. On a block schedule, combine two days per class. Pick your semester or enter the first day of class to see dates. Days off on the KCKPS calendar are skipped automatically.

Example videos

Watch real speeches before you write yours. Videos open on YouTube in a new tab. Notice what works and what you would do differently.

Research without a district account

Gale is our district database. Independent learners can use these free, credible tools. Always evaluate what you find.

  • Google ScholarSearch scholarly articles; look for free PDF links.
  • COREMillions of free, open-access research papers.
  • DOAJDirectory of peer-reviewed open-access journals.
  • PubMedHealth and medical research from the U.S. National Library of Medicine.
  • Library of CongressPrimary sources: photos, letters, recordings, newspapers.
  • National ArchivesHistorical government records and documents.
  • U.S. Census dataOfficial population and community statistics.
  • Pew Research CenterNonpartisan surveys on social issues and media.
  • Purdue OWL: APAHow to format APA 7 references (free, copyrighted guide).
  • Your public libraryA free library card often unlocks databases from home. Ask your local library.

About the author

Meet Mr. Bennett

Speech and Debate · Head Debate Coach, Wyandotte High School

13+ years teachingDebate KC 2026 High School Impact Coach of the Year

Steven Bennett is a Speech and Debate educator and Head Debate Coach at Wyandotte High School in Kansas City, Kansas. With over 13 years of teaching experience, he is passionate about helping students discover their voices, develop confidence, think critically, and become effective communicators.

He holds a bachelor’s degree in Speech Communication and Theatre Education from the University of Central Missouri and a master’s degree in Communication Studies from Arkansas State University. He is currently completing an Educational Specialist (Ed.S.) degree in Educational Technology at the University of Central Missouri.

Recognized as Debate KC’s 2026 High School Impact Coach of the Year, Mr. Bennett believes that public speaking is more than a classroom skill—it’s a life skill that opens doors to leadership, higher education, and meaningful community engagement.

Through this website, he hopes to make speech education accessible, engaging, and interactive for students and lifelong learners alike.

My educational philosophy

I believe education should empower students to discover their voices, think critically, and develop the confidence to communicate effectively. Learning should be active, meaningful, and accessible to every student, regardless of their background or abilities.

My classroom emphasizes learning by doing. Students grow through authentic experiences, collaboration, practice, reflection, and opportunities to improve. I believe technology, including artificial intelligence, should enhance learning rather than replace independent thinking, creativity, or human connection.

Above all, I believe education should open doors. Every student deserves meaningful opportunities to develop skills that extend beyond the classroom and prepare them for higher education, careers, leadership, and lifelong learning.

— Steven Bennett

About this resource

Speak Up | The Speaking Lab is a free, open resource designed to help learners discover their voices, build confidence, think critically, and communicate effectively. It is for high school students, college-level learners, teachers, and anyone who wants to speak with purpose.

Public speaking is a life skill you will use in…

  • EducationPresent research, lead discussions, and defend your ideas.
  • CareersInterview, pitch, train others, and speak up in meetings.
  • LeadershipInspire a team, explain a plan, and bring people together.
  • Civic lifeSpeak at a board meeting, advocate for your community, and vote informed.
  • Everyday lifeListen well, disagree respectfully, and say what you mean.
